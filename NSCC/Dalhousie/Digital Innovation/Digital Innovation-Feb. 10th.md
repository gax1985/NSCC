>   Digital Signage for CBC News 
>   He was the head of innovation for CBC news. He created the digital signage where CBC News signage would be posted everywhere, such as on mobile phones, on the bus and so on. The project was called **CBC News Everywhere**. Examples of its implementation would be museums and so on ... 

> ### Questions asked
> Would venues want our screens? 
> Would people see value or clutter? 
> How will multiple feeds co-play on the screen? 

People do not have strong imaginations when it comes to brainstorming. He made a mockup of the active screen, made by a *single PowerPoint slide*. The idea is that you would have a third of the screen dedicated to mobile, a row below the central box with scrolluing news and the right side covering the weather. 

#### The Winning Design

They decided on a all-encompassing box with the tv channel, with a thin vertical bar on the right side of the screen. You have to bottom horizontial bars that show news stories. 

#### What was surprising : 

The test audience did not mind the absence of *sound*. They did not mind that the bottom box showed rotating news stories instead of a scrolling bar. 

It helped within the news department and other departments like executive branch, marketing, IT and so on, as the mockup focused the dirextion before refining the implementation. Mockups gain support by making the product more real, and helps a lot with communications with executives and individuals who build the product, and the sales teams that are selling the product

## Manual-Firast Minimum Viable Product 

### Concierge 
Shows a person is handling some aspect of funtionaltity. Now, we have automation.

#### Example

**Food on the Table**

What they did is a *personal customized food delivery service* where ingredients are delivered weekly. What the founder did was he went door-to-door asking individuals about what they would and would not do. If they promised to deliver all the time, it gave them the validation they had real value. It is about proving the value. There is a *visible person*. 

### Wizard of Oz version

Conceals the person operating back-end functions

#### Example

**Zappos**

They tried to test the value of *shoe selection*, as it is a finicky area for people, where there are a lot of sizes, colors, variations and so on. There are so many variables to put together, so if you have a massive warehouse catering to all these different variations, this solves the issue. Zappos wanted to check and see if there is too much of a barrier of forgoing the in-person experience. They set up a working front-end. The founders went to the biggest shoe store they could find in town and told them they wanted to sell their shoes, and they will buy the shoes from the store, send it to the customer, and take their cut. They sold shoes they did not own, shipped it , collected their payment, and realized that there is real value here. The idea here is high-fidelity frontends where the client can make a purchase and all the work was done behind the scenes.

**User Story** :

They are very similar to **problem statement**, which tries to *find the job to be done*, where the **user story** will be at the level of the individual task/sub-goal versus the whole idea. 


**Problem Statement**
Talks about the overall problem

(vs User Story is situational. It is to *find a feature*)

Both the **problem statement** and **user stories** are about the results, *not the methods*. 


## Example User Story 

### ... right statement

    "As a time-starved student with multiple deliverables per month, I want to be reminded of upcoming deadlines so I can be confident I will get them done" 


vs ...

### ... *wrong* statement

    "As a busy student, I want to receive SMS notifications 48 hours before the deadline with hourly reminders in the final 6 hours so I can complete them". 


#### Why ? 

The wrong statement is quite specific about certain expectations, and it is cutting off the potential to do things. You are falling in love with the solution, not the problem. You are shutting off potential ways to do things better"

### ... *wrong* statement


    "I want to use a Google Calendar API integration to that I can sync my class chedule with my work shifts" 

### ... right statement

    "As a student with a part-time job, I want to see my class and work scheudules in one place so I can reduce stress about everything that is expected of me" 

We are shutting down possibilities, and losing a batter way of solving the problem.
